A stone order can meet its visual specification and still arrive damaged if handling and packing are not suitable. The correct packing method depends on **the stone format, weight, transport mode and handling equipment**.

## Packing is part of the technical specification

Large slabs often need purpose-designed supports or frames; cut-to-size pieces may use well-protected crates. The exporter and freight handler should confirm that the packing can be lifted, secured and handled safely across all transfers.

The aim is to manage movement, edge impacts, moisture exposure and the risks of repeated handling—not simply to make the packaging look robust.

## A practical pre-shipment list

1. **Inventory:** match slab or crate numbers to the approved packing list and photographs.
2. **Dimensions and mass:** record gross weight and package dimensions for loading plans.
3. **Separation:** check that faces and edges have suitable protective separators.
4. **Restraint:** verify that the package is stable and appropriately secured for the journey.
5. **Handling marks:** identify lifting points, orientation and fragile edges where relevant.
6. **Documentation:** confirm invoice, packing list, shipment details and any destination-specific certificates.

## Check the route, not only the container

A shipment may move from workshop to truck, terminal, vessel and destination warehouse. Each transfer changes the risk profile. Discuss the actual route, loading capability and insurance terms with the freight professional.

## Ask for evidence before dispatch

Request timestamped photographs of the **packed goods**, labels and final loading arrangement. If inspection is required, clarify who performs it and how findings are recorded. Never treat a generic packing photo as proof that a particular order is ready.

This guide is a buyer checklist, not a substitute for a certified load-securing plan or local customs and transport advice.
